What melatonin is, in one paragraph

Melatonin is a hormone your brain makes in response to darkness; it helps time your body's daily rhythm. Supplements are usually made in a lab. The NIH's complementary-health center (NCCIH) notes there is not enough strong evidence to recommend melatonin for chronic insomnia. In other words: it is a routine aid, not a treatment, and that's how we describe it here.

Why the format matters

Most melatonin in US stores is sold as gummies or tablets. A 2023 lab analysis published in JAMA tested 25 melatonin gummy products and found 22 were inaccurately labeled: one contained no detectable melatonin, and the rest ranged from 74% to 347% of the label amount. That finding is about gummies, not strips, and it doesn't tell us anything about any single strip brand. A printed amount is not proof of what's inside; the only real check is independent testing (such as a USP or NSF mark), which this seller does not claim. What this label does do is print every amount, with no proprietary blend.

Who should skip it, or ask first

  • Pregnant or breastfeeding: NCCIH notes there is a lack of research on safety.
  • Children and teens: talk with a pediatrician first; keep all melatonin out of children's reach.
  • Blood thinners or epilepsy: NCCIH says these situations need medical supervision.
  • Alcohol, sleeping pills or other sedatives: the strip contains valerian, which NCCIH says should not be combined with alcohol or sedatives.
  • Dementia: sleep-medicine guidelines cited by NCCIH recommend against melatonin for people with dementia.
  • Older adults: melatonin can stay active longer and cause next-day drowsiness.
  • Anyone with ongoing sleep trouble: see a clinician. For chronic insomnia, the recommended first treatment is CBT-I, not a supplement.

Reported side effects in short-term studies were mild: headache, dizziness, nausea and sleepiness. Don't drive or operate machinery after taking it.

Who it may suit

Adults who want a low, clearly labeled melatonin dose as part of a wind-down routine, who dislike swallowing pills or don't want candy-style gummies around the house, and who are fine paying about a dollar a night for the format.
